Background

As the lead ASEAN Member State on Protected Areas (PAs) for the ASEAN Working Group on Nature Conservation and Biodiversity, the Philippines is set to host the ASEAN Heritage Parks (AHP) Forum, aligning the crucial regional biodiversity agenda with the broader ASEAN 2026 Chairship theme, “Navigating Our Future, Together”. The AHP Programme is ASEAN's flagship initiative for biodiversity conservation, highlighting outstanding protected areas and promoting cooperation in their management. In partnership with the ASEAN Centre for Biodiversity (ACB), the AHP Secretariat (the Department of Environment and Natural Resources - Biodiversity Management (DENR-BMB) serving as focal lead), the Philippines will organise the AHP Forum to discuss alignment of regional conservation strategies, exchange best practices, and integrate AHP into the overarching goals of the Philippine Chairship. 

The AHP Forum aims to showcase and facilitate the exchange of best practices and innovative approaches in ASEAN Heritage Parks management. Part of its specific objectives includes highlighting the critical role of biodiversity corridors and transboundary conservation initiatives in maintaining ecological connectivity, ensuring resilient landscapes that support both wildlife and human food systems across borders. With the AHP Forum Theme of “Navigating our Future, Together: Harnessing ASEAN Heritage Parks for Resilience, Prosperity, and People Empowerment”, under Stream 1: AHP as an Anchor for Resilience, the topic on Ecological Connectivity is proposed as a breakout session (Breakout Session C) to highlight the essential role of biodiversity corridors and transboundary conservation initiatives in maintaining ecological connectivity, ensuring resilient landscapes can support wildlife movement, enhance ecosystem services, and sustain livelihoods across ecosystems and borders in the different ASEAN countries.

Objectives

Share and discuss experiences, lessons learned, good practices, innovations, failures and success stories on Integrated Ecosystems Management (IEM) of biodiversity corridors or connectivity conservation in ASEAN through efforts on species conservation, ecosystem restoration, stakeholder engagement, and livelihood;
Foster collaboration between different sectors working on integrated ecosystems management and connectivity conservation; and
Promote awareness and increase visibility on Corridor Projects across ASEAN

Expected Outputs

Increased understanding and appreciation of IEM of biodiversity corridors or connectivity conservation in ASEAN countries 
Strengthened collaboration between different sectors working on IEM and connectivity conservation

Background

The ASEAN Blue Economy Framework, adopted in 2023, recognizes the sustainable use of oceans, seas, and coastal resources as a key driver of inclusive economic growth, environmental sustainability, and regional resilience. As one of the world's most marine-dependent regions, ASEAN seeks to harness opportunities from sectors such as fisheries, aquaculture, tourism, maritime transport, renewable energy, and ecosystem conservation while ensuring that economic development remains within ecological limits. Achieving this vision requires decision-making that recognizes the true value of marine and coastal ecosystems and the benefits they provide to society.

“Capital has traditionally been thought of only as money, but capital describes any resource or asset that stores or provides value to people. Natural capital, social capital and human capital work in much the same way as traditional capital – if we invest in them they create value, and if we degrade them we limit their value.” (https://capitalscoalition.org/). 

It has come to the attention of governments that accounting for the contribution and degradation of natural capital is important in painting a more complete picture of where a country’s economy is headed, and whether this path is sustainable or not in the long-run. Human activities not only create an impact on natural capital, they also depend on the latter in varying degrees. The blue economy is no exception to this. 

The ASEAN is in the process of developing an ASEAN NCA Roadmap which contains a set of flagship programmes and relevant initiatives of companies and governments in the region that focus on economic activity as the catalyst of natural capital development. The Roadmap is being based on an assessment of natural capital policies and practices in ASEAN public and private sectors, including gaps and opportunities which aim to advance sustainable collective action and management of natural resources (https://asean.org/book/asean-natural-capital-status-report-investing-in-sustainable-natural-capital-in-asean/).

This session aims to highlight how Natural Capital Accounting can serve as an enabling tool for implementing the ASEAN Blue Economy Framework by providing evidence-based information to guide policy, investment, and sustainable resource management. Through practical experiences and regional perspectives, the session will explore how NCA can help ensure that blue economy development generates long-term economic prosperity while maintaining the health and productivity of ASEAN's marine and coastal natural capital.

Objectives

This session will attempt to provide a microcosm of these initiatives and show how NCA is being used in sustaining the blue economy. It will feature selected best practices that are being established for mainstreaming NCA into decision-making at the national and local levels, as well as how NCA relates to other blue economy policy frameworks that all aim to balance the impacts and dependencies of the various forms of capital on each other. 

Discuss enabling mechanisms needed by a key national government agency in implementing a law on NCA.
Share current efforts in mainstreaming NCA into local CRM planning.
Feature opportunities in enhancing investments in the blue economy in consideration of natural capital enhancementProvide a platform in harmonizing various marine spatial planning activities. 
Showcase best practices in transforming NCA results into revenue generating mechanisms and payments for ecosystem services.

Expected Outputs

Increased awareness on elements of enabling the national government in Mainstreaming NCA
Enhanced understanding on how to integrate NCA in the coastal resources management planning process, and other blue economy sectors
Identified opportunities in promoting investments in the blue economy without necessarily sacrificing natural capital
Convergence approaches for various Marine Spatial Planning (MSP) frameworks being implemented in the Philippines
Explore opportunities for advancing sustainable fisheries, renewable energy, and maritime transport through natural capital considerations

Background
ASEAN Heritage Parks (AHPs) are among the region's most ecologically significant protected areas, conserving globally important biodiversity while providing essential ecosystem services that support livelihoods, climate resilience, water security, and sustainable development. Despite their ecological and socio-economic value, many AHPs continue to face persistent financing gaps that limit their ability to effectively implement conservation, restoration, law enforcement, research, visitor management, and community development programs.
Traditional reliance on government appropriations and donor funding has proven insufficient to meet the growing financial needs of protected area management. As countries pursue the targets of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, there is increasing recognition that sustainable protected area management requires diversified and long-term financing strategies that combine public finance, private sector participation, community-based enterprises, innovative financial instruments, and cross-sector partnerships.
Across ASEAN, governments and protected area managers are developing innovative approaches to mobilize additional resources. These include conservation trust funds, biodiversity-related user fees, payment for ecosystem services, biodiversity offsets and credits, ecotourism enterprises, corporate partnerships, digital fundraising platforms, and blended finance mechanisms. Equally important are partnerships with local communities, Indigenous Peoples, civil society organizations, academic institutions, and the private sector, whose participation enhances not only financing but also stewardship, governance, innovation, and shared responsibility for biodiversity conservation.
The Philippines has developed several biodiversity finance solutions through the Biodiversity Finance Initiative (BIOFIN) and the Biodiversity Management Bureau (BMB), demonstrating how innovative financing and multi-stakeholder partnerships can strengthen protected area management while generating benefits for local communities. Sharing these experiences alongside initiatives from other ASEAN Member States will facilitate peer learning and encourage the replication and adaptation of successful approaches across the ASEAN Heritage Parks network.
Objectives
Highlight the importance of sustainable financing as a critical enabler of effective and resilient ASEAN Heritage Park management.
Showcase innovative biodiversity financing mechanisms and partnership models implemented by ASEAN Member States.
Share practical lessons, enabling policies, institutional arrangements, and success factors in mobilizing public, private, and community investments for protected area conservation.
Promote stronger collaboration among protected area managers, government agencies, development partners, civil society organizations, Indigenous Peoples and Local Communities (IPLCs), academia, and the private sector to support long-term biodiversity conservation.
Inspire ASEAN Heritage Parks to diversify their financing portfolios and strengthen partnerships that contribute to the long-term sustainability of protected area management.

Expected Outputs
Enhanced understanding of sustainable financing approaches and innovative partnership models applicable to ASEAN Heritage Parks.
Increased awareness of successful biodiversity finance solutions and partnership experiences from the Philippines and other ASEAN Member States.
Identified opportunities for adapting and replicating appropriate financing mechanisms within their respective protected areas.
Strengthened regional networks among protected area managers, government agencies, development partners, private sector organizations, and conservation practitioners to facilitate future collaboration.
Generated recommendations for strengthening sustainable financing and partnership engagement that can inform future ASEAN Heritage Park initiatives and capacity-building programs.

Background

The effectiveness and appropriateness of existing governance mechanisms highly influence how the ASEAN Heritage Parks’ (AHP) objectives are achieved. Exploration of more modern approaches for protected area conservation and people empowerment that combine science-backed strategies, traditional knowledge, and policy with innovative social marketing techniques have now become increasingly essential. With these concepts and practices of governance within protected areas constantly evolving and being enriched over time, the components shaping the success of these frameworks significantly grow complex. Stakeholder awareness, capacities, rights, power dynamics, responsibility sharing, costs and benefits, and even the availability of sustainable support from the government, communities, academe, civil society organizations, and the public, among many others, also come into play. 

Given this rapidly changing landscape, there is a need to continuously re-evaluate and deepen our understanding of governance within heritage parks, and the underlying relationships between key actors, our environment, and society as a whole, to co-create more effective, adaptive, and sustainable AHP governance mechanisms.

Objectives

This interactive Session aims to reinforce the participants’ understanding of the role of science, tradition, policy, behavioral change, and creative public engagement in the long-term conservation of AHPs by:

Harvesting innovations, proven good practices, and other key experiences on AHP and natural resource management; 
Exploring how scientific research, traditional knowledge, and policy work together in strengthening governance models within AHPs; and 
Highlighting innovative social marketing techniques that transform institutional and community mindsets, and foster lasting behavioral shifts.

Expected Outputs

Session documentation capturing key insights from resource persons and participants will be made available after the Conference.

Session Format

Stream 3 - Breakout Session A will employ non-traditional, interactive, and light-hearted knowledge exchange formats to ensure the sustained attention, easier appreciation, and active engagement of all participants.

Topic 1: The Role of Science, Tradition, and Policy in Strengthening AHP Governance Frameworks

Resource Persons
RPs for Topic 1 are from mandated agencies, academe, or are community leaders and policy-makers, who can provide expert guidance and perspectives on AHP management. 

Mechanics (Gamified Case Study Presentation)
Using a randomizer, each RP will be assigned a scenario with a protected area governance challenge. Within six (6) minutes, the RP will share highlights of their specific science-based, traditional, and/or policy interventions and governance mechanisms that successfully navigate the scenario and help address other interconnected issues observed within AHPs. 

After all the resource persons’ presentations, participants will be asked to offer their own experiences or insights by responding to this focus question through a “Harvest Wall” entry: “Aside from science, tradition, and policy, what other key elements could strengthen protected area governance frameworks in your country or project sites?” The floor will also be opened for a quick Q&amp;A portion.

Pre-work Needed
RPs will be requested to prepare short stories or responses to a list of scenarios or guide questions in advance. RPs will address only one random scenario during the live session. Resource persons may also provide a maximum of two (2) slides with related photos to show during their turn. A quick briefing and rehearsal with the RPs will be arranged prior to the Session, if necessary. 

Topic 2: Transformation of Institutional and Community Mindsets Towards Inclusive AHP Governance

Resource Persons
RP for Topic 2 will share stories of local leaders or People’s Organizations that have grown from being passive, unempowered community members into active decision-makers who drive positive behavioral change and sustainable conservation actions within their AHPs and communities.

Mechanics (Guided Storytelling) 
Each RP begins their presentation by showing a “then and now” snapshot. For 30 seconds, participants will be briefly invited to guess the key elements that could have made the change possible. The RP will then reveal three (3) key milestones, turning points, and/or important factors during the “in-betweens” that led to the transformation. Each RP will have seven (7) minutes total for the entire presentation.

Following the presentation, the participants will be prompted to share their own community empowerment story by answering this focus question on the Harvest Wall: “Given the emerging threats and climate events, what specific improvements or behavioral change in relation to protected area governance will enable AHP communities to adapt to such changes?” A few questions from the audience will also be accommodated.

Pre-work Needed
RPs will be requested to prepare one (1) “before and after” slide in advance. RPs may also opt to prepare a maximum of three (3) additional slides to support the three “in-between” stories. A quick briefing and rehearsal with the RPs will be arranged prior to the Session, if necessary. 

Considering the roster of RPs for Topic 2, assistance for the preparation of presentation material and other needs (e.g., translation) will also be extended.

Topic 3: Advocacy and Public Engagement to Amplify AHP Conservation

Resource Persons
RPs for Topic 3 are well-known artists, content creators, and advocates who leverage social media and innovative storytelling strategies to engage the broader public in conservation.

Mechanics (Media Showcase) 
RPs will be given a total of six (6) minutes each to showcase one (1) creative output (e.g., illustration, video, photo, audio, publication, sculpture, etc.) that best encapsulates the power of art and storytelling for conservation, and to deliver a quick spiel on how AHP communities and partners can leverage art to broaden their reach, enhance awareness, and rally support for AHP conservation efforts.

Once the RP showcase has ended, participants will be invited to answer the last question on the Harvest Wall: “What AHP-related issue or conservation effort deserves more attention?” RPs will also entertain a few questions.

Pre-work Needed
RPs will be requested to prepare any creative material to showcase in advance. A quick briefing and rehearsal with the RPs will be arranged prior to the Session, if necessary. 

Closing Activity: Commitment Setting

Mechanics (Social Media Posting)
Participants will be grouped by country. Each group will be instructed to co-create a shared commitment to advance AHP governance in their country. Groups must post their pledge and key Session takeaways publicly on social media channels using the official Conference and Session hashtags. All submissions will be featured on-screen. The group with the highest audience impact will win a special prize.

Background
Indigenous and traditional knowledge systems have guided the stewardship of biodiversity, ecosystems, and natural resources across ASEAN for generations. Rooted in long-standing relationships with nature, these knowledge systems encompass ecological knowledge, customary governance, cultural values, and resource management practices that have sustained both biodiversity and community well-being.
As ASEAN seeks to strengthen the management of ASEAN Heritage Parks and other protected and conserved areas, there is growing recognition that effective conservation depends not only on scientific knowledge, but also on the meaningful recognition, respect, and application of Indigenous and traditional knowledge. Consistent with a rights-based approach to conservation, Indigenous Peoples and local communities are not only custodians of valuable knowledge, but also partners in the governance, management, and long-term stewardship of biodiversity.
Building on this session’s plenary discussion on Conservation and Human Rights, this forum will showcase experiences from across ASEAN that demonstrate how Indigenous and traditional knowledge contributes to biodiversity conservation, protected and conserved area management, community stewardship, and sustainable livelihoods. Through regional case studies and an interactive dialogue, the session will facilitate knowledge exchange, highlight good practices, and explore opportunities to strengthen the integration of Indigenous and traditional knowledge into the governance and management of ASEAN Heritage Parks and other protected and conserved areas.

Objectives
Highlight Indigenous Peoples’ leadership and community-driven approaches in achieving effective and equitable biodiversity conservation. 
Demonstrate the contributions of Indigenous Peoples and Local Communities to achieving global biodiversity goals. 
Foster regional dialogue, promote cross-country learning and partnerships, and identify opportunities to advance inclusive and effective conservation in protected and conserved areas. 

Expected Outputs
Greater recognition of Indigenous Peoples and local communities as rights-holders, knowledge holders, and conservation leaders, and of their contributions to the effective and equitable governance of ASEAN Heritage Parks and other protected and conserved areas.
Enhanced understanding of regional good practices and lessons learned on harnessing Indigenous and traditional knowledge, customary governance, and community stewardship to strengthen biodiversity conservation and protected area management.
Key messages and recommendations for promoting the meaningful recognition and integration of Indigenous and traditional knowledge, alongside scientific knowledge, in the governance and management of ASEAN Heritage Parks and other protected and conserved areas.
Strengthened regional collaboration and peer learning among Indigenous Peoples and local communities, protected area managers, government agencies, and conservation partners to advance inclusive, rights-based, and locally led conservation across ASEAN.

Format
The plenary should set the conceptual foundation on Conservation and Human Rights.
The case presentations provide concrete examples and evidence.
The panel discussion then synthesizes these experiences into broader regional and policy insights. This creates a more engaging discussion because panelists can refer to the presentations rather than speaking in the abstract.

Plenary Discussion 

Conservation and Human Rights: Advancing Indigenous Peoples' and Local Communities' Leadership in Protected and Conserved Areas by Sushil Raj, Executive Director of WCS Rights and Communities

Effective biodiversity conservation and the realization of human rights are mutually reinforcing. Across the globe, Indigenous Peoples and local communities have long stewarded some of the world's most biologically important landscapes and seascapes through their knowledge systems, customary governance, and enduring relationships with nature. As the global community advances the implementation of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, there is growing recognition that achieving conservation goals depends on respecting the rights, agency, and leadership of Indigenous Peoples and local communities.

This plenary presentation will explore the evolution of rights-based conservation and its implications for the governance and management of protected and conserved areas, including ASEAN Heritage Parks. It will examine how conservation can move beyond participation toward meaningful recognition of Indigenous Peoples and local communities as rights-holders, knowledge holders, and conservation leaders. The plenary will provide the conceptual foundation for the forum by highlighting the opportunities, challenges, and pathways for integrating human rights, Indigenous and traditional knowledge, and inclusive governance to achieve effective, equitable, and enduring conservation outcomes.

Background
ASEAN Heritage Parks (AHPs) are among Southeast Asia's most important protected areas, safeguarding globally significant biodiversity while supporting the livelihoods, cultures, and well-being of millions of people. Achieving effective and sustainable management of these protected areas requires more than strong ecological science—it also depends on meaningful participation by local communities, women, youth, volunteers, researchers, and civil society.
Across ASEAN, women and young people are increasingly emerging as environmental leaders, educators, innovators, and advocates for biodiversity conservation. At the same time, citizen science has become an important tool that enables communities, volunteers, students, and local organizations to contribute directly to biodiversity monitoring, ecosystem restoration, and conservation decision-making.
Building on the ASEAN Heritage Parks Forum theme, &quot;Navigating a Sustainable Future for ASEAN Heritage Parks,&quot; this breakout session will showcase successful initiatives that demonstrate how inclusive participation and capacity development strengthen protected area governance. Through experiences of various organizations and local communities from the ASEAN Member States, participants will explore practical approaches for empowering women, engaging youth leadership, promoting citizen science, and building partnerships that contribute to long-term biodiversity conservation.
The session aims to inspire greater collaboration among governments, protected area managers, academia, civil society organizations, Indigenous Peoples and Local Communities (IPLCs), volunteers, and youth networks to ensure that everyone can contribute meaningfully to the future of ASEAN Heritage Parks.

Objectives
 Showcase innovative approaches, successful experiences, and lessons learned on women and youth engagement in biodiversity conservation and protected area management across ASEAN.
 Highlight  how citizen science and meaningful participation of youth, women, and IPLCs strengthens biodiversity monitoring and protected area management.
Facilitate dialogue among governments, NGOs, academia, youth organizations, communities, and other forum attendees to generate practical recommendations to strengthen inclusive participation in ASEAN Heritage Parks.

Expected Outputs
At the end of the breakout session, participants are expected to:
Better understand inclusive conservation approaches.
Learn practical models for youth leadership and women's empowerment.
Gain knowledge of citizen science tools applicable to protected areas.
Identify recommendations for strengthening inclusive participation within ASEAN Heritage Parks.

Session Format
The session will combine case study lightning talks, moderated panel discussions, and audience reflection to promote knowledge sharing and interactive dialogue on inclusive participation and capacity development in ASEAN Heritage Parks.
Opening Plenary
The session will begin with opening preliminaries led by the host, followed by a Message from the Co-Organizer delivered by Hanns Seidel Stiftung. The Session Chair will then officially open the breakout session, followed by an Inspirational Keynote Address by Dr. Jerome Montemayor, Executive Director of the ASEAN Centre for Biodiversity (ACB), to set the context for the discussions.

Topic 1: Engagement of Women and Youth in Conservation
The first thematic session will be conducted in a fishbowl discussion format. The session will begin with the invited speakers seated in the inner circle, where they will engage in a facilitated conversation on their experiences, challenges, innovative approaches, and lessons learned in promoting the meaningful engagement of women and youth in biodiversity conservation. The facilitator will guide the discussion with key questions while encouraging dialogue among the speakers.
As the discussion progresses, participants from the audience will be invited to join the conversation by taking the available seat in the fishbowl, allowing them to share perspectives, ask questions, or contribute relevant experiences. This interactive format is designed to foster inclusive dialogue, peer learning, and active participation between speakers and participants.
A 15-minute coffee break will follow.
Topic 2: Enhanced Stakeholder Engagement through Citizen Science
The second thematic session will begin with a series of lightning case study presentations, where each speaker will have five minutes to showcase innovative citizen science initiatives and practical experiences. The presentations will be followed by a moderated panel discussion and audience Q&amp;A, allowing participants to explore common challenges, lessons learned, and opportunities for scaling citizen science across ASEAN Heritage Parks.
Closing Reflection
The session will conclude with a brief audience reflection and synthesis of key messages and recommendations, highlighting practical actions to strengthen inclusive participation and capacity development across ASEAN Heritage Parks.
