ACTIVITY DESIGN
ASEAN HERITAGE PARKS FORUM

“Navigating a Sustainable Future for ASEAN Heritage Parks”

September 8-10, 2026
Seda Vertis North, Quezon City

Background and Rationale

The Philippines is a biodiversity powerhouse that possesses a remarkable gamut of ecosystems, species and genetic resources. It holds more than two-thirds of the planet’s biological wealth and natural capital, making it one of the world’s 17 megadiverse countries (Biodiversity Management Bureau, DENR 2014).

To safeguard and conserve the country’s invaluable biodiversity and natural resources, legal measures and programmes continue to be instituted and implemented. In 1987, the strategy of establishing an “integrated protected area system” was adopted as part of the comprehensive efforts to preserve biodiversity (La Viña et al. 2010). Subsequently, in 1992, Republic No. 7586 or the National Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS) Act of 1992, provided a legal framework for the establishment and management of a national system of protected areas. 

As the lead ASEAN Member State on Protected Areas (PAs) for the ASEAN Working Group on Nature Conservation and Biodiversity, the Philippines is set to host the ASEAN Heritage Parks (AHP) Forum, aligning the crucial regional biodiversity agenda with the broader ASEAN 2026 Chairship theme, “Navigating Our Future, Together”.

The AHP Programme is ASEAN’s flagship initiative for biodiversity conservation, recognizing protected areas of outstanding biodiversity importance and promoting regional cooperation in their effective management. In partnership with the ASEAN Centre for Biodiversity (ACB), the AHP Secretariat, the Philippines will organize the ASEAN Heritage Parks (AHP) Forum as a platform for learning, collaboration, and innovation among AHP managers, community leaders, policymakers, development partners, and other stakeholders. The Forum will facilitate the exchange of best practices, strengthen regional cooperation, discuss the alignment of conservation strategies and priorities, and contribute to the integration of the AHP Programme into the broader goals of the Philippine ASEAN Chairship.

Distinct from the traditional ASEAN Heritage Parks (AHP) Conference, which is convened every three years and focuses primarily on technical discussions, knowledge sharing, and institutional cooperation, the AHP Forum adopts a more people-centered approach by placing communities at the heart of conservation efforts. It will highlight the critical role of AHP managers, Indigenous Peoples, local communities, women, and youth as stewards of protected areas, recognizing that long-term conservation success depends on empowered and engaged communities. The Forum will also showcase Philippine ASEAN Heritage Parks and their experiences in protected area management, biodiversity conservation, sustainable livelihoods, and inclusive governance.

Furthermore, the Forum will provide a venue for AHP managers and stakeholders to contribute practical insights and recommendations toward the refinement and operationalization of the ASEAN Heritage Parks Operations Manual. Through these discussions, the Forum seeks to strengthen the link between regional policies and on-the-ground conservation practice, ensuring that the AHP Programme remains responsive to emerging challenges and opportunities across ASEAN.

Objectives

The AHP Forum aims to showcase and facilitate the exchange of best practices and innovative approaches in ASEAN Heritage Parks management, particularly highlighting Philippine experiences, to strengthen the effective and sustainable management of protected areas across ASEAN Member States. Specifically, the Forum aims to: 

Demonstrate how AHPs and Nature-based Solutions (NbS) contribute to climate resilience and food security, addressing the interconnected challenges of biodiversity loss, climate change, and pollution.

Highlight the critical role of biodiversity corridors and transboundary conservation initiatives in maintaining ecological connectivity, ensuring resilient landscapes that support both wildlife and human food systems across borders.

Mainstream biodiversity by positioning AHPs as vital natural capital and drivers of the blue economy, while fostering private sector partnerships and securing investments to sustain protected areas that underpin key economic sectors.

Promote inclusive governance by integrating traditional knowledge, supporting sustainable livelihoods and biodiversity-friendly enterprises, and ensuring the active leadership of women, youth, Indigenous Peoples, and local communities in PA and AHP management.

Impacts

The ASEAN Heritage Parks Forum 2026 is expected to generate meaningful and lasting impacts by strengthening regional cooperation, enhancing the capacity of stakeholders, and advancing innovative and inclusive approaches to protected area management. Through knowledge exchange, partnership-building, and strategic dialogue, the Forum will contribute to more resilient ecosystems, sustainable economies, and empowered communities across ASEAN. Key impacts of the forum includes the following: 

AHP managers and stakeholders will have enhanced capacity to apply adaptive and science-based management approaches, resulting in more effective, evidence-informed, and responsive protected area governance.

Regional collaboration and South-South cooperation among ASEAN Member States will be strengthened, resulting in improved knowledge sharing, coordinated actions, and more harmonized approaches to biodiversity conservation.
Expanded partnerships and resource mobilization will result in increased investments supporting biodiversity conservation, ecosystem restoration, and sustainable use of natural resources across AHPs.

Biodiversity considerations will be more effectively integrated into development planning and decision-making, resulting in greater recognition of AHPs as natural capital that supports sustainable economic growth and climate resilience.

Indigenous Peoples, local communities, women, and youth will be more actively engaged and recognized as key partners, resulting in more inclusive governance and strengthened community-based conservation and livelihood opportunities.

Strategic and evidence-based recommendations will be adopted, resulting in strengthened policy direction and improved implementation of the ASEAN Heritage Parks Programme at both national and regional levels.

Back-to-back Activities and Side Events

2nd ASEAN Green Initiative (AGI) Recognition Event 

The 2nd AGI Recognition Event aims to recognise native tree planting initiatives across ASEAN, while also facilitating a substantive discussion to identify critical issues, challenges, opportunities, and recommended actions necessary for advancing forest rehabilitation and broader ecosystem restoration efforts at both national and regional scales. 

Launch of AHP Regional Action Plan

Official launch of the AHP Regional Action Plan (AHP RAP) which provides standardised guidelines, procedures, and best practices for the designation, management, and monitoring of AHPs. Accompanying this is the comprehensive Regional Action Plan, outlining strategic priorities, collaborative activities, and measurable targets for the AHP Programme across the region over a defined period. 

Launch of Small Grants Program III

Aims to launch the Small Grants Programme III (SGP III). It will be important to note that In the Philippines has been included in the third iteration of the SGP. The Balinsasayao Twin Lakes Natural Park, a recent Philippine AHP, is one of the sites in the ASEAN for the implementation of SGP III. The Programme is being administered by the ACB alongside third-party implementers, including the German Development Bank, Kreditanstalt für Wiederaufbau (KfW).

Launch of Philippine AHP Network

The establishment of the Philippine ASEAN Heritage Parks Network (PAN) will strengthen collaboration among Philippine ASEAN Heritage Parks (AHPs) by providing a national platform for coordination, knowledge exchange, peer learning, and collaborative action. The Network will promote the sharing of best practices, enhance monitoring and reporting, and support protected areas pursuing ASEAN Heritage Park designation.

The PAN will also strengthen the contribution of Philippine AHPs to the implementation of the Philippine Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan (PBSAP), the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (KMGBF), and other biodiversity commitments. Through science-based conservation, nature-based solutions, climate resilience, and inclusive governance, the Network will further position Philippine AHPs as models of excellence in biodiversity conservation and protected area management.

Ceremonial Signing of the Month of the Protected Areas (MOPA) Joint Endorsement

The Month of the Protected Areas (MOPA) builds on the momentum of the 2026 Year of the Protected Areas (YOPA) campaign by institutionalizing an annual nationwide celebration to raise public awareness and support for protected areas. Led by the DENR in partnership with the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and the Department of Tourism (DOT), the initiative aims to promote biodiversity conservation, sustainable tourism, and stronger multistakeholder collaboration in protected area management. The ceremonial signing of the Joint Endorsement will exemplify the shared commitment of the three agencies to advancing the cause and strengthen inter-agency cooperation toward its institutionalization.

Launch of the Biodiversity Finance Project under the Global Biodiversity Framework Fund

The DENR BMB and UNDP initiated Phase II of the BIOFIN Project to build capacity and support various finance solutions. The project aims to increase public and private sector biodiversity investments and reduce financial flows with incidental environmental impacts to support the KMGBF-aligned Philippine Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan (PBSAP) targets. The Project entitled &quot;Philippines Biodiversity Financing Program&quot;, was approved by the Global Environment Facility (GEF) under the first programming tranche of the Global Biodiversity Framework Fund (GBFF). It shall be implemented from July 2026 to July 2032. The Special Presidential Authority was approved by the Office of the President, with its Project Document signed by the DENR Secretary and UNDP this July 2026.  With the Project now entering its implementation phase, preparatory activities are currently underway, including the conduct of the National project launch and ceremonial signing.

This is in keeping in tune with the theme of the Forum, it is worth noting that the AHP Programme  is one of ASEAN’s flagship programmes where the ACB is the Secretariat.

Workshop on AHP Programme Operations Manual - In line with the official launch of the AHP RAP and the finalisation of the AHP Operations Manual (AHP Ops Manual); a  capacity enhancement workshop will be held for all AHP Managers and other relevant stakeholders in the region aim to provide guidance in order for participants to enhance their understanding and effectively implement the guidelines set in the AHP Programme Operations Manual in their respective AHPs and AMS.

Youth Empowerment Co-design Lab
An intergenerational workshop that will bring together youth delegates, policymakers, ASEAN Heritage Park managers, conservation practitioners, and development partners to strengthen meaningful youth participation in biodiversity conservation and the management of ASEAN Heritage Parks. Using the World Café methodology, participants will engage in facilitated small-group discussions to exchange perspectives, identify opportunities for collaboration, and co-develop practical recommendations for advancing youth leadership and inclusive biodiversity governance within the ASEAN Heritage Parks Programme.

In line with the official launch of the AHP RAP and the finalisation of the AHP Operations Manual (AHP Ops Manual); a  capacity enhancement workshop will be held for all AHP Managers and other relevant stakeholders in the region aim to provide guidance in order for participants to enhance their understanding and effectively implement the guidelines set in the AHP Programme Operations Manual in their respective AHPs and AMS.

Youth Empowerment Co-design Lab
An intergenerational workshop that will bring together youth delegates, policymakers, ASEAN Heritage Park managers, conservation practitioners, and development partners to strengthen meaningful youth participation in biodiversity conservation and the management of ASEAN Heritage Parks. Using the World Café methodology, participants will engage in facilitated small-group discussions to exchange perspectives, identify opportunities for collaboration, and co-develop practical recommendations for advancing youth leadership and inclusive biodiversity governance within the ASEAN Heritage Parks Programme.
